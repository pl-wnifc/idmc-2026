Title: 

RISM Online: Delivering digital musicology data at scale

Abstract:

With over 1.6 million source records, 2.5 million incipits, 160,000 people, and 26,000 institutions, RISM Online delivers this data to more than 500,000 visitors each month in seven languages. As one of the largest open bibliographic resources for musical sources, RISM Online increasingly serves not only as a discovery platform, but also as a foundation for computational and data-driven musicological research.

This talk will begin with a high-level overview of key features of RISM Online, including its incipit search engine, multilingual interfaces, and dedicated searches for national and institutional collections.

The presentation will then discuss several underlying architectural features that support interoperability, extensibility, and long-term sustainability. These include the integration of authority data, persistent identifiers, and semantic data models that enable connections between musical works, sources, people, and institutions across collections.

Finally, the talk will preview ongoing experimental work on the development of a public Linked Data platform delivering more than 300 million RDF triples. This work aims to provide researchers and institutions with new opportunities for data reuse, network analysis, and cross-collection discovery, while also positioning RISM within a broader ecosystem of open cultural heritage and digital humanities infrastructures.

Bio: 

Dr. Andrew Hankinson specializes in web technologies for building scalable and usable search and retrieval systems and currently leads development for RISM Online at the RISM Digital Center. He holds a Master’s in Library and Information Studies from McGill University, and a PhD in Music Information Retrieval from McGill University. He was a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Oxford, where he worked on the Digital Image Archive of Medieval Music (DIAMM) project, and served as Senior Software Engineer for Digital Collections Discovery at the Bodleian Libraries.
